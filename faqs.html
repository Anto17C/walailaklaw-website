Criminal Law & Bail

Bail in Thailand allows a defendant to be temporarily released while awaiting trial. The court determines the amount of security required based on the seriousness of the offence and flight risk. Bail may be granted in the form of cash, assets, or a guarantor. Read more.
Yes. Foreign nationals have the same fundamental right to apply for bail as Thai citizens, though courts often require higher security amounts and may impose travel restrictions.
Authorities may detain a suspect for up to 48 hours for investigation before requiring a court order for further detention. You are entitled to legal counsel during this window.

Civil & Family Law

The duration depends on complexity, court schedules, and whether mediation resolves the dispute early. Some cases conclude within months, while more complex matters may take longer. Read more.
Marital property acquired during the marriage is generally divided equally under Thai law, subject to any prenuptial agreement in place.
Custody decisions are based on the best interests of the child, considering each parent's circumstances and ability to provide care.

Business & Investment Law

Foreign ownership may be restricted under the Foreign Business Act. However, certain exceptions apply, including Board of Investment (BOI) promotion or specific treaty privileges. Proper legal structuring is essential. Read more.
A common benchmark is 2 million THB in registered capital per foreign work permit, alongside required Thai employee ratios.

Generally not freehold. Foreigners can, however, hold property through several lawful routes. The table sets out what each route allows and the main limit to check before you pay anything.

RouteCan a foreigner hold it?What to know
Freehold landGenerally noThe Land Code (s.86) restricts foreign acquisition of land to narrow statutory exceptions, such as certain approved investments. It is not a route for an ordinary buyer.
Condominium unitYes, within the quotaForeigners may hold up to 49% of the total unit area of a registered condominium (Condominium Act). The purchase funds must be remitted from abroad in foreign currency, with the bank evidence kept for the Land Office.
House or buildingYes, the structureA foreigner may own a building, but not the land beneath it. The right to occupy the land needs its own legal basis, such as a lease, usufruct or superficies.
Registered leaseYesA lease of immovable property cannot exceed 30 years (Civil and Commercial Code s.540) and must be registered at the Land Office. Renewal clauses are only as strong as the contract and the registration.
UsufructYesA registered right to use and take the benefit of land, granted for life or for a fixed term of up to 30 years (CCC s.1417). It is personal to the holder and ends with the term or on death.
SuperficiesYesA registered right to own structures on another person's land (CCC s.1410), for a fixed term or for life. Its transferability and inheritance depend on the terms registered.
Thai companyOnly if genuinely Thai-controlledA company whose shares or voting power are held half or more by foreigners is treated as foreign for land purposes. Nominee shareholders breach the Foreign Business Act and put the property at risk.
Land in a Thai spouse's nameNo, the land belongs to the spouseA foreign spouse has no ownership, and the Land Office commonly asks the foreign spouse to declare that the funds are the Thai spouse's separate property. Protection has to be arranged separately.

General information as of October 2026, not legal advice. Thresholds and Land Office practice change, so have the specific property and structure checked before you commit.
